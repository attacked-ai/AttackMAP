Volkswagen recalls over 208,700 US vehicles over steering rack bolt defect

On September 18, 2026, the US National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) announced that Volkswagen is recalling 208,724 vehicles in the United States because a steering rack mounting bolt may corrode and break. The recall covers certain 2018 Volkswagen Tiguan, 2018-2019 Volkswagen Atlas and 2019-2021 Audi Q3 vehicles. NHTSA tracks the campaign as 26V590000, and Volkswagen's internal recall codes are 48LG and 48VT. The defect is in one of the bolts that attach the steering rack to the vehicle subframe. Corrosion can weaken the bolt until it breaks. If it breaks, the steering rack housing can also break, which can cause a loss of steering control and raises the risk of a crash for occupants and other road users. VINs covered by the recall became searchable on NHTSA.gov on September 16, 2026, two days before the public announcement. As the remedy, dealers will replace the right-side steering rack mounting bolt free of charge. Owner notification letters are expected to be mailed on November 10, 2026. Owners can reach Volkswagen customer service at 1-800-893-5298. The sources did not report any crashes, injuries or deaths linked to the defect, and they did not say whether warranty claims or field reports prompted the recall. Reuters reported the recall first, and business and automotive outlets carried it the same day, citing NHTSA.

Volkswagen works council warns up to 115,000 jobs at risk as restructuring advances

Volkswagen's Group and Central Works Council, chaired by Daniela Cavallo, warned that as many as 115,000 jobs could ultimately be lost under a worst-case reading of the carmaker's restructuring. The council reached the figure by adding three tranches: roughly 50,000 reductions already agreed with the supervisory board under the December 2024 future-wage settlement targeting 2030; a further group-wide cut of about 50,000 positions approved by the supervisory board on 3 September 2026 under the "Future Plan 2030," of which roughly 25,000 fall on Germany; and up to 40,000 more posts at four plants whose long-term future is unresolved. Those four sites are Volkswagen's Emden, Zwickau and Hanover factories and Audi's Neckarsulm plant. Volkswagen's own statement conceded that, staggered from 2031 to 2034, no competitively viable follow-up production can currently be guaranteed at them; the supervisory board ordered a competitive production concept for the four by 30 June 2027 and said alternative uses are being explored. The driver is structural overcapacity — VW says European capacity exceeds demand by more than 500,000 units — alongside Chinese competition, tariffs and a target of about 9 million vehicles a year. Management ranks are to shrink from roughly 21,500 to 16,000. A fifth site, Osnabrück, is on a separate path: Lower Saxony and investor Aurelius signed a statement of intent to take it over and convert it to defence production, retaining roughly 1,200 to 1,400 of 1,800 jobs. Volkswagen disputed the works council's arithmetic, saying no plant closures have been decided and that 115,000 is a hypothetical ceiling rather than a confirmed programme. IG Metall invoked the consultation clause in the 2024 agreement, forcing formal talks on employment security and the sites' future, which Volkswagen agreed to join. Reports put provisioning for the cuts and closures at about 16 billion euros. Closures would require a two-thirds supervisory-board majority, which labour representatives and Lower Saxony can block.

Stellantis recalls over 97,300 Jeep Wagoneer and Grand Wagoneer SUVs over detaching roof-rack crossbars

On September 17, 2026, FCA US LLC (Stellantis North America) notified dealers of a safety recall covering an estimated 97,349 full-size Jeep SUVs whose factory roof-rack crossbars may detach while the vehicle is being driven. The population includes select 2022-2025 Jeep Wagoneer, 2022-2026 Jeep Grand Wagoneer, 2023-2025 Wagoneer L and Grand Wagoneer L models (one report gives the Grand Wagoneer L range as 2023-2026). The vehicles were built between February 24, 2021 and October 30, 2025. The defect is a manufacturing fault. According to the recall documents filed with the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA), the pivot bolts that secure the crossbars to the roof rack may not have been tightened to the correct torque during assembly. Over time these bolts can back out completely, and a crossbar that separates from the roof rails at speed becomes road debris that endangers following traffic. Owners may notice a rattling noise or a missing bolt. The crossbar assembly is supplied by Minth - Plastic Trim International. FCA opened its investigation on June 25, 2026 and had identified at least 15 related warranty claims up to May 29, 2026. No crashes or injuries have been reported. Under the remedy, authorised Chrysler and Jeep dealers will inspect the crossbars and replace the factory pivot bolts with new bolts of a different design, free of charge. Affected VINs became searchable on NHTSA's recall lookup on September 17, 2026. Interim owner notification letters are due to be mailed from October 8, 2026, and a second notice will follow once remedy parts are fully stocked. Owners can contact Chrysler customer service on 1-800-853-1403.

The population is 215,433 2021-2023 Grand Cherokee L, 97,477 2022-2023 Grand Cherokee and 15,471 2022-2023 Grand Cherokee 4xe (PHEV), built between December 5, 2020 and May 31, 2023; air-suspension vehicles are excluded. That yielded recall 26V051 (20D) on January 29, 2026, covering 80,620 vehicles. Complaints persisted: between July 7 and July 29, 2026 NHTSA forwarded additional VOQ complaints and met with FCA, and FCA opened a third investigation on July 24, 2026 into springs detaching after both prior repairs. As of August 6, 2026 FCA counted 29 customer assistance records and 406 warranty claims received between July 15, 2023 and July 28, 2026, and no accidents or injuries. FCA's Vehicle Regulations Committee declared the defect on August 26, 2026. The new remedy replaces both rear coil spring lower isolators (part 68668244AA, supplier Airboss, Auburn Hills) free of charge; vehicles already repaired under 23V413 or 26V051 must return. Dealers were notified September 9, 2026, the date VINs became searchable on NHTSA.gov; interim owner letters begin September 29, 2026, with a final remedy notification to follow.

NHTSA opens safety investigation into Tesla Cybercab self-certification

Tesla began charging passengers for rides in its purpose-built Cybercab robotaxi in Austin, Texas on September 3, 2026, launching at a small, quiet downtown event attended by shareholders and influencers and offering trips through its Robotaxi app. The two-seat Cybercab has no steering wheel, no brake or accelerator pedals, and no mirrors; 45 units were registered in Texas at launch. Within roughly a day, on September 4, the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration opened Audit Query AQ26002 covering approximately 1,000 Cybercab units. Because US manufacturers self-certify compliance with Federal Motor Vehicle Safety Standards rather than obtaining pre-approval, the audit examines the process and technical data Tesla relied on when certifying the vehicle — in particular the extent to which Tesla determined that FMVSS provisions written around permanently attached conventional manual controls (steering wheel, pedals, mirrors) were inapplicable to a vehicle that has none. NHTSA Administrator Jonathan Morrison said the agency "fully supports the safe development and deployment of automated vehicles," but that as the federal regulator it needs to ensure all of its laws are followed. Coverage contrasted Tesla's unilateral self-certification with Zoox, which self-certified in 2022, drew an NHTSA investigation that delayed its commercialization for years, and ultimately secured a Part 555 exemption in July 2026 — capped at 2,500 vehicles annually — before beginning paid rides in Las Vegas. An Audit Query is an information-gathering tool rather than a finding of violation; NHTSA can close it without action or escalate toward a formal compliance or defect investigation. No crash, injury, or confirmed safety defect is attached to it. Tesla shares fell 5.92% on September 4, closing at $354.08, with analysts also underwhelmed by the launch itself. NHTSA has separately said it aims to complete updates to FMVSS for vehicles without manual controls in the coming months, after the administration proposed removing the manual-control requirement in June 2026.

Volkswagen board approves 50,000 more job cuts under 'Future Plan 2030' restructuring

On September 3, 2026, Volkswagen AG's supervisory board unanimously approved CEO Oliver Blume's "Future Plan 2030," which VW calls the most strategically far-reaching transformation programme in its history. It cuts roughly 50,000 further positions group-wide including management roles, doubling the reductions agreed since late 2024 and taking the cumulative total toward 100,000. VW had already agreed about 35,000 cuts with IG Metall in December 2024, then raised the German target to roughly 50,000 in March 2026 after 2025 net profit fell 44% to EUR 6.9 billion and the operating margin sank to 2.8%. The underlying failure is structural overcapacity meeting a collapsed China business. European group capacity exceeds demand by more than 500,000 units; first-half 2026 China deliveries fell 26.1% to 971,000, the lowest since 2010; Blume says support-function overhead runs over 30% above comparable rivals; and US tariffs (15% on EU-built cars, up to 27.5% on Mexican-built) compound the squeeze. Targets include 9 million annual sales and a 9% operating margin (about EUR 31 billion operating result) by 2030, overhead cut to EUR 37 billion, EUR 135 billion of capex and R&D over 2027-2031, the model range halved by 2035 and complexity down roughly 75%. Labour leaders Daniela Cavallo and Christiane Benner said they had "prevented a dangerous escalation," blocked a core-brand carve-out, and stressed employment protection runs to end-2030; IG Metall calls the 50,000 a cost-derived theoretical figure rather than a binding target. NHTSA's record lists roughly 2,071 potentially affected model-year 2026-2027 VNL (4) and VNR (4) units; trade coverage of the August 29 announcement cited approximately 1,851 model-year 2027 VNL and VNR trucks. Lucid Motors recalls 27,185 Air EVs over fire-risk lighting circuit fault

On August 20, 2026, Lucid USA, Inc. filed a Part 573 Safety Recall Report with NHTSA (campaign 26V540, internal number SR-26-06-0) covering 27,185 Lucid Air sedans built between October 11, 2021 and July 27, 2026, spanning model years 2022 through 2026 — effectively every Air the company had delivered. NHTSA published the filing on August 28, prompting widespread coverage. The defect is in software rather than hardware: the control logic for the eFuse governing the Air's low-voltage exterior lighting circuits provided insufficient overcurrent protection, allowing a circuit to remain energized at excess amperage. Sustained overcurrent could overheat the wiring and produce smoke or fire, and could also disable exterior lamps, reducing visibility and raising crash risk. Warning signs listed in the filing include smoke, a burning odor, or lighting-malfunction telltales. The chronology begins in June 2023, when a fire in a Lucid-owned MY2023 Air started at the front left corner; a third-party expert established no definitive root cause but could not rule out the front combination lamp. A March 2025 fire in a MY2022 Air likely originated in the front left light blade controller. A third fire, reported by a customer in a MY2024 Air in May 2026, triggered a Product Safety Working Group review. That investigation also identified four smoke incidents between October 2025 and April 2026 traced to overheating of the center high-mounted stop lamp (CHMSL) circuit, plus 33 warranty reports of CHMSL wire harness damage. No injuries or deaths were reported. While the investigation was open, Lucid released software version 2.10.0 over the air in July 2026, lowering the eFuse amperage threshold and disabling retry logic that could re-energize a faulted circuit within the same key cycle. On August 13, 2026 Lucid's Product Safety Executive Council determined a safety-related defect existed and adopted 2.10.0 as the remedy. Telematics showed 20,719 vehicles already updated at filing, leaving 6,466. Owners are advised to park outside away from structures until updated; mailed notification is due October 16, 2026, with reimbursement available.
